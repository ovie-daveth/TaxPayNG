## 1. **INDIVIDUAL TAXATION - Additional Topics**

### Currently Covered:
- Basic tax reform overview
- Freelancer/creator taxation
- Tax reliefs (basic)
- Tax calculation basics

### Can Be Added from Act:

#### A. **Tax Brackets & Rates (Detailed)**
- **Section 58**: Exact progressive tax brackets
  - First ₦800,000: 0%
  - ₦800,000 - ₦3,000,000: 15%
  - ₦3,000,000 - ₦12,000,000: 18%
  - ₦12,000,000 - ₦25,000,000: 21%
  - ₦25,000,000 - ₦50,000,000: 23%
  - Above ₦50,000,000: 25%

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "What are the exact tax brackets for individuals?"
A: "The new tax reform uses progressive brackets: First ₦800,000 is tax-free, then 15% on income up to ₦3 million, 18% up to ₦12 million, 21% up to ₦25 million, 23% up to ₦50 million, and 25% for income above ₦50 million."
```

#### B. **Employment Income & Benefits-in-Kind (Section 13-14)**
- When employment income is considered derived from Nigeria
- Benefits-in-kind taxation
- Housing benefits and rental value
- Transport allowances (up to ₦30,000/month exempt)

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "How are benefits-in-kind like housing and transport taxed?"
A: "If your employer provides housing at no cost or below market rent, you're taxed on the annual rental value. Transport allowances up to ₦30,000/month (₦360,000/year) are tax-exempt. Any excess is taxable."
```

#### C. **Chargeable Gains (Capital Gains Tax) - Section 33-55**
- **Currently NOT covered at all**
- What constitutes chargeable gains
- Exemptions (principal private residence - once per lifetime, personal chattels, motor vehicles, gifts, compensation for personal injury up to ₦50 million)
- Chargeable assets definition

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "Do I pay tax on selling my house or car?"
A: "Capital gains on your principal private residence are exempt once per lifetime. Personal chattels (items under ₦5 million) and up to two motor vehicles used privately are also exempt. However, if you sell property or assets used for business, you may have capital gains tax obligations."
```

#### D. **Personal Injury Compensation (Section 51)**
- Compensation up to ₦50 million is exempt
- Only excess amount is taxable

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "Is compensation for personal injury taxable?"
A: "Compensation for personal injury, job loss, or wrong suffered up to ₦50 million is completely tax-exempt. Only amounts exceeding ₦50 million are subject to capital gains tax."
```

#### E. **Deductions & Reliefs (More Detailed)**
- **Section 30-31**: Detailed eligible deductions
- Interest on housing loans
- Research and development deductions
- Bad debts provisions

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "Can I claim deductions for interest on housing loans?"
A: "Yes. Interest paid on loans for developing your own owner-occupied residential house is deductible from your taxable income, in addition to rent relief (20% of rent capped at ₦500,000)."
```

## 2. **BUSINESS TAXATION - Additional Topics**

### Currently Covered:
- Small business exemptions
- CIT rates (basic)
- VAT thresholds
- Development levy

### Can Be Added from Act:

#### A. **Small Company Definition (More Precise)**
- **Section 56**: Small companies pay 0% CIT
- Turnover ≤ ₦100 million AND total assets ≤ ₦250 million
- Effective tax rate calculation for larger companies

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "What exactly defines a small company?"
A: "A small company must have annual turnover not exceeding ₦100 million AND total fixed assets not above ₦250 million. Both conditions must be met. Such companies pay 0% Company Income Tax."
```

#### B. **Development Levy Details (Section 59)**
- 4% levy on assessable profits
- Distribution breakdown:
  - Tertiary Education Trust Fund: 50%
  - Nigerian Education Loan: 15%
  - NITDA Fund: 8%
  - NASENI: 8%
  - NBTI: 4%
  - Defence & Security: 10%
  - Cybersecurity Fund: 5%
- Small companies EXEMPT

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "How is the Development Levy used?"
A: "The 4% Development Levy supports national infrastructure: 50% goes to Tertiary Education Trust Fund, 15% to Nigerian Education Loan, 8% each to NITDA and NASENI, 4% to NBTI, 10% to Defence & Security Infrastructure, and 5% to Cybersecurity Fund. Small companies are fully exempt."
```

#### C. **VAT Regulations (Section 144-158)**
- **Currently basic coverage**
- Taxable supplies at 7.5% (or 0% for certain items)
- VAT invoice requirements
- Input tax credits
- Exempt supplies (agriculture inputs, medical supplies, etc.)

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "What VAT rate applies, and which items are exempt?"
A: "VAT is charged at 7.5% on taxable supplies. However, agricultural inputs (fertilizers, machinery, diesel), medical supplies, educational materials, and certain exported goods are exempt or charged at 0%. Small businesses (turnover < ₦100 million) don't charge VAT at all."
```

#### D. **Specialized Industries**
- **Insurance Business (Section 61)**: Different taxation rules
- **Lottery & Gaming (Section 62)**: Special deductions
- **Mining Operations (Section 64)**: Special provisions
- **Collective Investment Schemes (Section 63)**: Tax on distributions

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "Are there special tax rules for insurance or mining businesses?"
A: "Yes. Insurance companies are taxed differently based on whether they offer life insurance, general insurance, or both. Mining operations have special provisions for deductions and capital allowances. These industries require specialized tax handling."
```

#### E. **Export Processing & Free Trade Zones (Section 60)**
- Tax exemptions for eligible entities
- Conditions for qualification

**New FAQ Entry:**
```
Q: "Do Free Trade Zone businesses pay tax?"
A: "Export processing and Free Trade Zone entities that meet specific conditions enjoy significant tax exemptions, including CIT exemptions and reduced VAT obligations, to encourage export-oriented businesses."
```
